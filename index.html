About the Compendium

Launched at the WTO's Fourteenth Ministerial Conference (MC14) in Yaoundé, at a side event on 25 March 2026, the Compendium is a joint initiative of the Informal Working Group on Trade and Gender and the Informal Working Group on MSMEs. It was proposed in a joint communication of 27 February 2026 (WT/MIN(26)/4 • INF/TGE/COM/13/Rev.1 • INF/MSME/W/68/Rev.1) by the Co-Chairs of the Informal Working Group on Trade and Gender, Ambassador Ana Patricia Benedetti (El Salvador), Ambassador James Baxter (Australia) and Ambassador Clara Delgado (Cabo Verde), and the Coordinator of the Informal Working Group on MSMEs, Ambassador Matthew Wilson (Barbados). It supports the implementation of the IWG Work Plan 2025–2026 (INF/TGE/W/10/Rev.3) and the MSME Group Coordinator's Report (INF/MSME/W/64/Rev.1).

The content of the Compendium comes from a survey. Between June and October 2026, WTO Members answered a survey on their policies, initiatives and programmes supporting women in digital trade, and other WTO stakeholders answered a shorter survey of their own. Each response, answer and question shown here is taken from that survey, quoted exactly as written by the respondent.

Objectives and Scope

Map policies, initiatives and programmes

The Compendium maps WTO Members' and Observer Governments' trade policies, initiatives and programmes focusing on supporting women, including women-led MSMEs, in all trade-related aspects of digitalisation, including e-commerce and trade in technology products.

Reflect Members' priorities

Its scope reflects Members' priorities and objectives. It covers trade policies that support women in digitalisation, such as export preparedness and support programmes and e-commerce and gender chapters in free trade agreements, as well as other policies that affect women's ability to engage in digitalisation and trade, such as education and skills training, workforce participation, access to technology, cybersecurity, e-payment systems and access to finance.

Themes

The survey for WTO Members covers six themes, plus a final question for additional comments, good practices and references. WTO stakeholders answered a dedicated survey of twelve questions.

1Strategies and Priorities
Whether women are included in national digital strategies, the dimensions of digitalisation that most influence their participation, the sectors offering the greatest opportunities and challenges, and Members' priority objectives and actions.
2Initiatives, Instruments and Market Access
Programmes supporting women in digital trade, the areas of intervention they cover, trade policies with digitalisation-related components, the instruments used and the market access support available to women entrepreneurs.
3Skills and Capacity Building
Training on digital entrepreneurship, e-commerce and artificial intelligence, the skills covered, train-the-trainer approaches and their impact, and capacity building with international or regional organizations.
4Coordination and Actors
How policies on women, trade and digitalisation are coordinated across government, the governance mechanisms in place and the actors involved in supporting women in digital trade.
5Data, Monitoring and Evaluation
Collection of sex-disaggregated data on digital trade, the types of data and institutions involved, data on barriers and market access, and how programmes are monitored and evaluated.
6Trade Facilitation, Innovation and Finance
Initiatives linking digital infrastructure with trade facilitation, the inclusion of women in innovation-driven trade initiatives, and support for investment, start-up funding, incubators and accelerators.
